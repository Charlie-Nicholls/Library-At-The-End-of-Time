### Text

> [!recite|clean no-t]
>	The Library at The End of Time is in fact no such thing. Time doesn't really end after all, it's just that after a couple hundred billion years all the interesting things have already happened. Every few million years, somewhere in the universe, one particle might bump into another and if there were anyone left to see it this would doubtless be considered the height of entertainment and be met with loud 'whoops' and clapping. But, while the audience has long since left, time keeps on ploughing pointlessly forwards. Trying to set up a library at the limit of infinity, when even time has given up, would be ridiculous - the commute alone would render the endeavour doomed. No, it would be more accurate to say the library exists a little to the left of time and a few hours anticlockwise of space in a realm known as The Abstract. But apparently that was "confusing and way too long a name. And besides 'The End of Time' is more enigmatic and we've got to consider the branding".
>
>	But whenever it is, The Library contains every story ever told, every book ever written, and all the knowledge ever recorded. It is in a very real sense, a reflection of the people of the material plane. Consequently, The Library maintains a constant connection to the material plane, allowing books to leave and enter The Library is essential for its continued existence. As such, the sects associated with The Library will freely lend books of lesser importance to the public. Books which are considered dangerous for a given time or place require special dispensation to be lent out, and their return is more strictly enforced. Textbooks of technology in the wrong time could be devastating, a mislaid historical record could swing a war, even a story told to the right person could start a revolution.
